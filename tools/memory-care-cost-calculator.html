Memory Care Cost Calculator

Prime Domain Capital Research · November 19, 2026

The number on the brochure is never the number on the bill. A memory care community quotes a base rate, then adds level-of-care charges, medication management, and incontinence care — and the total lands 20% to 30% above the assisted living rate in the same building. This calculator starts from the surveyed assisted living median in your state and applies that documented premium, so the estimate you get is the all-in monthly figure families actually pay — not the teaser rate.

Medicaid asset limits vary by state — most states set the individual limit near $2,000 in countable assets, but the exact figure, the home exemption, and the spousal allowance all differ. Spending down to the limit does not guarantee eligibility, and the rules on transfers and look-back periods are strict. Treat this line as a planning signal, not eligibility advice: talk to an elder-law attorney in your state before making decisions about assets.

How the numbers work

The base figure is the surveyed assisted living median for the state you pick: the CareScout Cost of Care Survey for 2025, based on 4,944 completed surveys of licensed assisted living communities, with fieldwork from July to November 2025 and results released March 2, 2026. The national median is $6,200 a month for a private one-bedroom, or $74,400 a year.

Memory care is not surveyed as its own line. The industry’s working rule, from the National Center for Assisted Living, is that memory care runs 20% to 30% above standard assisted living in the same market, reflecting higher staff ratios, secured environments, and dementia-trained staff. This calculator applies that band: the low end of the range is the state median plus 20%, the high end is plus 30%, and the midpoint — plus 25% — is the single figure used for the spend-down estimate. At the national median, that puts memory care at $7,440 to $8,060 a month, with $7,750 as the midpoint.

Two independent surveys land in the same range from different directions. A Place for Mom’s 2026 data puts memory care at $6,690 a month against $5,419 for assisted living inside the same survey — a 23% premium, consistent with the industry rule. SeniorLiving.org’s June 2026 figures put the national memory care median at $8,019 a month. The spread between those two — $6,690 to $8,019 — brackets the calculator’s national range, which is the point: no single number is the price. The range is the honest answer.

What the calculator does not include: the community fee at move-in ($2,000 to $5,000 in many contracts), second-person fees, and level-of-care add-ons that rise as needs increase. Those sit on top of the medians here, which is why families should always ask for the all-in monthly figure, in writing, before deciding.

The cheapest state is Mississippi at $4,369 a month for assisted living; the priciest is Hawaii at $12,096. The gap between them is 2.8 times — and the same care, priced the same way, costs $5,243 to $5,680 a month as memory care in Mississippi and $14,515 to $15,725 in Hawaii.

Why the state matters more than the diagnosis

Families usually shop memory care as if the care level sets the price. The survey says geography sets it first: the difference between the cheapest and priciest states is larger than the difference between assisted living and memory care in any single state. A family in Mississippi paying the top of the memory care band still pays less than a family in Hawaii paying the assisted living median. That is why the first question in any memory care budget is not how much care is needed. It is where.

The counterargument is worth stating plainly. State medians hide the variation inside a state — a community in San Francisco or Manhattan can charge double the state median, and a rural community can charge well under it. The median is a planning benchmark, not a quote. But the benchmark is still the right starting point, because it is the only figure that is comparable across all fifty states, and it is the figure the rest of the senior-care ecosystem already cites.

Memory care typically lasts two to three years, and the tail runs to ten. At the national midpoint of $7,750 a month, two years costs $186,000 and three years $279,000 — before community fees and care-level increases. That is the arithmetic that makes the spend-down line above the most important output on this page: most families do not run out of care needs. They run out of the money set aside to pay for them.
